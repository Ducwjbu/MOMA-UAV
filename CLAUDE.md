# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project

MOMA-AC: a preference-conditioned, multi-objective TD3 agent that controls a UAV base station in a simulated network-slicing environment (eMBB/URLLC/mMTC). It learns a Pareto front over three objectives: QoS, energy, and fairness. The research question is whether smarter weight selection (OLS, GPI-LS, GPI-PD) beats the original MOMA-AC's uniform omega sampling, which is the baseline. Those comparisons are on one UAV. `ma3` is the paper's multi-agent MOMA-TD3 on three UAVs, with uniform sampling only so far. Since 2026-11-06 the three-UAV problem is the main one. Its proposed method is `ma3_prior`, a residual policy on a formation prior with gamma 0.9, and plain `ma3` is its baseline.

## Architecture

The data flow is `train_moma_ac.py` (loop, metrics) ↔ `agents_moma_ac.py` (agent) ↔ `environment.py` (simulator). From `utils.py`, only `Configuration` is used.

**Environment step (`NetworkSlicingEnv.step`).** Takes `{uav_id: action}` and runs: apply pose and power → move UEs (and hotspots without a `traffic_map`) → associate UEs to UAVs → rebuild demand areas → apply bandwidth → allocate RBs → refresh SINR, throughput, queue, delay, and PER → compute rewards. Each step represents `T_L` = 60 simulated seconds. The default config has one UAV. `done` is true as soon as any UAV's battery runs out, or, with a `return_home` section (both configs), falls to the energy it needs to fly back to the Home Point. `info` also reports `coverage`, `handovers` for that step, `min_uav_distance` (inf with one UAV), and `backhaul_limited` (share of UAVs over their backhaul capacity). The handover-rate observation counts handovers in the last `T_L`, so it falls to 0 once they stop.

**Radio model.** Transmit power is split equally across the RBs in use, so SINR is per RB and noise is per RB (`noise_psd + NF + 10log10(rb_bandwidth)`). Rate is attenuated Shannon, capped at `max_spectral_efficiency`. Numerology is NR n78: 3.5 GHz, 273 RBs of 360 kHz.

**Several UAVs (`config/environment/ma3.yaml`: 3 UAVs, 91 arriving UEs plus 26 RSUs).** All UAVs reuse the same band. Interference from UAV j is its expected power on a victim's RB, `P_j / total_rbs` (zero if j uses no RBs). Without an `uav_params.antenna` section it is reduced by `sidelobe_attenuation_db` at UEs outside j's beam cone (without that key they get none), and a UE joins the nearest UAV whose cone contains it. `min_uav_separation` pushes UAVs apart after all moves, inside the fly ranges and with no reward penalty, and propulsion energy uses the final positions. A push can exceed the speed limit: a search found pushes up to about 56 m per step (a coincident pair overshoots by 50 m, 950 m against 900 m), while the team's mean speed is unaffected. A handover interrupts `D` = 50 ms of the 60 s step, so it adds the mean delay `D^2 / (2 T_L)` and scales the 1% failure loss by `D / T_L`, instead of charging every packet of the step, which makes a handover nearly free in the reward. The reward vector stays one shared team reward. None of this changes results with one UAV. A `fleet` section (thesis, absent in both configs) makes the active UAVs change: `num_uavs` is the fleet size, and at each reset `_draw_fleet` makes a random subset of `n_active_min` to `num_uavs` UAVs active; with `withdraw_prob` one active UAV leaves at a step uniform in `withdraw_steps` (a fault, or a battery swap). An inactive UAV keeps its slot in the observation and action: its action is ignored, it serves no UE (association masks it), causes no interference, draws no energy, is left out of separation, `done`, the energy objective's mean and `min_uav_distance`, and other UAVs see zeros for its dx, dy, dh and power. `info['n_active']` reports the count. A v2 `shift` section (TIP 4; set in `v2.yaml`, can't be combined with `fleet`, needs `return_home`) makes an episode a `duration_steps` window (240) of a running operation and its end a terminal `done` (the `return_home` end rule no longer applies). `num_uavs` is then the number of serving positions (slots of the observation and action, 10 in `v2.yaml`), and airframes are separate env state: `_start_shift` draws S in `n_serving` (2-10) positions in random slots, S + ceil(`spares_per_uav` S) airframes, and a depot of `pairs_per_station` full pairs per station (ceil(`stations_per_uav` S), or `station_count`); serving batteries are staggered between the relay threshold and full. `_shift_update` runs after each step's reward: airframes in transit count down (legs timed and charged by `_home_leg`, the energy-and-duration version of `_home_leg_energy`), the depot charges one pair per station on the CC-CV curve of `shift.charge` (fullest first, as the BS60), a returned airframe swaps in `swap_steps` and takes the fullest pair if it reaches `launch_soc`, else waits; an arriving spare takes over its position (same place, its battery; the old airframe flies home), a serving airframe at `_leave_threshold` (return leg + `_step_energy_bound`, the most one step can draw) leaves anyway and its position goes empty (inactive, as in `fleet`) until an airframe arrives; ready spares go first to empty positions, then to positions below `_relay_threshold` (leave threshold + the steps out + 1 at the position's recent power draw, an exponential average in `shift_state['power']`, plus `relay_power_margin`; a higher draw only makes the relay late). The policy steers positions only; the observation adds the share of the shift left (1 dim); the energy objective stays the mean over serving positions, and transit energy goes to `info['transit_energy_j']` with `relays`, `gaps`, `waiting_battery`, `depot_mean_soc`, `n_serving_step`. The prior reassigns vertices when the serving set changes. `v2.yaml` fits the propulsion model to the M300 at its 9 kg MTOW (W 88.26 N, 4 x 21 in rotors, P0 fitted so the battery lasts 31 min at hover: hover 1061 W, 27.7 min with the radio at full power, against Table I's 1.37 kW in `ma3.yaml`; `docs/v2/sources.md`). With 1.5 BS60 and 0.5 spare per position (the `v2.yaml` defaults) no position was empty in `docs/v2/check_tip4.py`; with 1 BS60 per position about 5% of the time. A position is relayed about every 26 min hovering at full power.

**Realistic link model (both env configs; `default.yaml` is `ma3.yaml` with 1 UAV on a 1155 m area, a third of ma3's, with a third of its UEs and RSUs, and no separation).** Parameters and sources are in the comments of the env configs. Each part is off when its key is absent, which restores the older model.
- `city` (v1a): `city.py` builds an explicit ITU-R P.1410 grid (pitch 57.7 m, footprints 40.8 m, Rayleigh heights) from the seeded global RNG at every `reset`. `_update_channel_state` runs once per step after the UE moves: LoS of every UE-UAV link is geometric (`City.line_of_sight`, exact segment-box test, in numba; `line_of_sight_numpy` is the older vectorized version, with identical output on 60k test segments; an indoor UE's own building is skipped), UEs inside a footprint are indoor with the O2I loss of TR 38.901 Table 7.4.3-3, and shadowing (`channel.shadowing` sigma_los/nlos/o2i) is an AR(1) over the distance the UE plus the UAV moved, with Table 7.5-6 decorrelation distances. State lives on the UE object (`los`, `indoor`, `shadow_db`, `o2i_db`, `k_factor_db`). The backhaul's LoS is geometric too. UAVs are kept `roof_clearance` above the roof under them (`_apply_height_bounds`, after the separation push). Geometric LoS is far below Al-Hourani's S-curve for street-level UEs (street canyons), so coverage is much lower than with the statistical model.
- `channel.fast_fading`: the rate is the mean of the attenuated Shannon rate over Rician (outdoor LoS, K per UE-UAV link) or Rayleigh fading, from a table built at init with a fixed seed (`_build_fading_table`). Only with the city.
- `wind`: Dai et al. (arXiv:2304.06909). One draw per episode (`_draw_wind`), power law in height. The configs use Hanoi: Weibull from Global Wind Atlas (district mean), direction from the Lang station wind rose (`direction_frequencies`, 16 sectors; without the key, von Mises). Both configs set `rotor_speed: air`. The commanded ground velocity is cut so the airspeed stays within `velocity_max` (`_limit_airspeed`); the UAV stays below the height where the wind reaches `max_speed`, and an episode too windy even at the lowest height is redrawn (`wind_rejections`). Propulsion uses Dai's Eq. 8 at zero acceleration; `rotor_speed: ground` follows the paper (blade and induced power at ground speed), `air` uses the airspeed. Descent is limited by `velocity_max_descent`.
- `uav_params.antenna`: the 3GPP TR 38.901 element pattern pointing down, `8 - min(12 (theta/65)^2, 30)` dBi, on serving and interfering links. A UE joins the UAV with the highest reference SNR (`P / total_rbs` per RB at the UAV's current power), and is uncovered below `min_association_snr_db` (-7 dB, where the rate model reaches CQI 1). A serving UAV is kept unless another beats it by `handover_hysteresis_db` (event A3, 3 dB assumed). The rate is `0.75 log2(1 + SINR / SNR_eff)` (Mogensen et al., `snr_efficiency_db` 1.25), capped at CQI 15 and 0 below CQI 1 (`min_spectral_efficiency`). SINR then comes from `_sinr_db` in numpy, not the numba function.
- `channel.shadowing`: per step and UE-UAV link, the LoS state is drawn from Al-Hourani's `P_LoS`, eta takes that state's value, and log-normal shadowing of TR 36.777 UMa-AV is added (`sigma_LoS = 4.64 exp(-0.0066 h)`, `sigma_NLoS = 6` dB). Association and SINR share the step's draws (`channel_draws`). Without it eta is the expectation over the LoS state, as before.
- `a2g` is read from the env config by `_link_gains_db` and `_backhaul_capacity`. `ma3` uses Al-Hourani's dense urban values.
- `energy_models.radio_static_power` switches the radio draw to EARTH's micro BS model, `112 + 2.6 P_tx` W, instead of `P_tx / pa_efficiency`.
- Propulsion uses Table I of the arXiv version of Zeng et al. (W = 100 N, hover 1.37 kW), with a 548 Wh battery (two DJI TB60), 17 m/s, 6.3 W, NF 9 dB, and heights 50-120 m (civil limit). The battery lasts about 22 min at hover. `return_home` (both configs) models the M300's Low Battery RTH (User Manual v1.8, p. 22: the threshold comes from the altitude and the distance to the Home Point): the Home Point is the gateway's ground station, `_home_leg_energy` is level flight at `velocity_max` in the episode's wind then the descent (radio off), the flight out is charged at reset (`charge_outbound_flight`, also after `eval_formation` places its UAVs), and the episode ends when a UAV's battery reaches its return energy. Episodes are 18 steps for `ma3` and 19 for one UAV: the longest missions that still leave the return energy when hovering at full power at the farthest point, the worst case over wind speeds. The energy-optimal speed (about 21 m/s) is above the speed limit.

**Backhaul (the `backhaul` config section; absent means unlimited).** Each UAV has its own out-of-band link to one ground gateway. Without `model`, it uses the access A2G and rate models, so capacity falls with distance. With `model: mmwave` (both configs), it follows Galkin et al. (arXiv:1710.10807): path loss `c d^-alpha` by LoS state, their Eq. 3 for `P_LoS` between two elevated ends, 64.2 dB of antenna gain, and the capacity is the mean over the LoS state. It almost never binds (`backhaul_limited` is 0 for the fixed triangle), unlike the earlier assumed 20 MHz link. Load is `sum(min(access rate, offered load)) / capacity`. Above load 1 all the UAV's UEs slow down by the factor `1 / load`, which is continuous at load 1 and keeps the shares within a UAV. The queue's service rate is `min(total UE rate, capacity)` at every load, so delay and drops rise as load approaches 1. Individual UE rates can still exceed the capacity, because the queue carries the penalty rather than throughput satisfaction. With a backhaul the queue also differs in three ways, none of which apply without one: packet size is the mean over packets with no 1000-bit overhead, so `rho` is offered bits over service bits; delay is capped at the 99 ms overload value; and the finite-buffer drop formula also runs above `rho = 1`, so drops are continuous there. The gateway is at the area's edge, 30 m high. Backhaul transmit power isn't in the energy objective. With the earlier 20 MHz link, fixed triangles still did best centered in the area, so don't assume a backhaul pulls UAVs toward the gateway. With one UAV, `info` and the observation have it but `eval_metrics.csv` has no `backhaul_limited`.

**Observation.** One UAV, 80 dims: 5 UAV state (position, power, battery) + 9 DAs × 7 features + 4 handover + 8 surroundings. N > 1 UAVs, `30 + 4(N-1)` dims (38 for 3): the constant one-hots go, leaving 9 DAs × 1 feature, and uncovered UEs per direction (4) plus each other UAV's dx, dy, dh, and power ratio (4 each) are added. A `backhaul` section adds 1 dim, the UAV's load capped at 2 and halved, and a `wind` section adds 2 (the wind vector at the UAV's height over `max_speed`), so the configs have 83 (one UAV) and 41 (`ma3`).

**Action: 13 dims per UAV.**
- `[0:2]` are horizontal deltas, scaled by `velocity_max * T_L` (the vector is capped at `velocity_max * T_L`, 1020 m per step, and the airspeed limit can cut it further). `[2]` is the altitude delta, scaled by `velocity_max_vertical * T_L`.
- `[3]` is power. The actor outputs `[0, 1]` and the env clips to `[0.05, 1]`.
- `[4:13]` are bandwidth fractions per DA, in `_form_demand_areas` order (slice eMBB/URLLC/mMTC × distance Near/Medium/Far). They are scaled down if the total exceeds the available RBs.

The actor uses tanh for the first 3 dims and sigmoid for the rest. `select_action` and `update` clip to these same ranges, so keep them in sync.

v2 `action.mode: slice_share` (absent in both v1.1 configs; set in `v2.yaml`): 7 dims, `[4:7]` one share per slice (eMBB, URLLC, mMTC), and the trainer takes `act_dim` from `env.act_dim`. `_allocate_rbs_slices` then replaces `_allocate_rbs_fairly` and ignores `rb_sharing`: for each UE with traffic, a serving UAV and a non-zero rate, `_provisional_rb_rates` gives the rate of one RB if every busy UAV used all its RBs (which the scheduler makes true), and the need is `ceil(demand / (target load x rate))`. `_scheduler_target_loads` takes per slice the largest load whose M/M/1/K drop rate is a tenth of the failure budget left after the link residual (v2: eMBB 0.75, URLLC 0.93, mMTC 0.95); serving exactly the offered load puts the queue at load 1. `_schedule_slices`: the shares (scaled to sum 1 if above) are guarantees, filled by water-filling (equal RB counts capped at the need, which is proportional fairness with fixed rates and a load cap); unused guaranteed RBs go to slices with unmet need by share, the rest to all UEs with traffic, so no RB is idle while a UE has traffic. No random numbers besides one RB permutation per UAV. Demand areas keep their UEs for the observation. On the hovering triangle with even shares (`docs/v2/check_tip2.py`, 10 validation episodes) the SLA shares go from 0.212 / 0.148 / 0.450 (9 even DA fractions, 17% of RBs idle) to 0.327 / 0.353 / 0.450; a step takes about 1 ms more (18.6 ms against 15.8 ms for `ma3.yaml`).

**Traffic and slices (v1b).** Each step `_update_ue_traffic_patterns` sets every UE's `demand_bps`, `packet_bytes` and `packet_rate`: eMBB 50 Mbps with probability 0.2, else idle (TS 22.261 Table 7.1-1); URLLC 10 Mbps; mMTC TR 45.820 periodic reports (about 0.06 bps). URLLC users are fixed road-side units (`rsu` section, `_add_rsus`, `ue.fixed`): every 1 km along a TR 36.885 road grid, snapped to street crossings, never departing; arriving UEs are only eMBB and mMTC. UEs without demand get no RBs and are left out of QoS and fairness. Queue, delay, PER, backhaul load and fairness all read the per-UE fields. eMBB counts are set so the mean offered load equals the fleet capacity (see the slicing comments). With the city, outdoor UEs walk along streets at 3 km/h, indoor UEs stay put, UE height 1.5 m. Where arriving UEs appear (v1.1): with a `traffic_map` section (both configs), `traffic_map.py` draws at every `reset` the log-normal, spatially correlated density of Lee et al. 2014 (IEEE Wireless Commun. 21(1), Eq. 1-2, Table 1 urban DL: sigma 2.3991, omega_max 0.011592 rad/m, 10 m pixels), redrawn until its coherence distance is within 30 m of 71.4 m (about one redraw per reset), and holds it for the episode. eMBB UEs (`traffic_map.slices`) are placed with probability proportional to it and mMTC uniformly; there are no hotspots then. mu only scales the density, so it is left out and the load calibration stands. With sigma 2.4 about a third of the eMBB density sits in 1% of the pixels. Without the section, the older hotspots (2 at reset, 70% of arrivals near one). Each UAV uses a random subset of RBs (`rb_order`), matching the interference model. Two thesis keys under `slicing`, off in both configs (with them off the env output is bit-identical to `v1.1`): `rb_sharing` (`none`, `idle`: RBs reserved for a demand area with no UE that has traffic go to the UAV's demand areas that have some, in proportion to what they reserved; `all`: the RBs the action left unreserved too, so the action's shares act as minimum guarantees like `rRMPolicyMinRatio` of TS 28.541), in `_share_free_rbs`; and `per_slice_queues` (one M/D/1, M/M/1/K queue per slice at each UAV with a third of the buffer, `_queue_delay_drop`, `_queue_of`). On the hovering triangle with bandwidth split evenly over the 9 demand areas (10 validation episodes) `idle` raises the URLLC SLA share from 0.25 to 0.40 and eMBB from 0.024 to 0.038, and per-slice queues raise mMTC (0.33 to 0.45) but cut URLLC (0.25 to 0.12). With the trained `ma3_prior` policy, which gives URLLC most of the bandwidth (QoS corner, 2 seeds x 10 validation episodes), per-slice queues instead raise URLLC from 0.42 to 0.48 (0.50 with `idle`) and mMTC from 0.40 to 0.44, and lower eMBB (about 0.02 to 0.01): isolation protects the slices the policy funds.

**Rewards.** The agent trains on `info["reward_vector"]` = `[qos, -energy, fairness]`, each in a unit range and unnormalized by the agent. The scalar reward returned by `step` and `reward_weights` in the env config do not affect training.
- v2 `objectives` section (absent in both v1.1 configs; `v2.yaml` has `[sla_embb, sla_urllc, sla_mmtc, energy]`): the reward vector is the named entries, in order (`env.objective_names`). `sla_<slice>` is the mean over the slice's UEs with demand of `sigmoid(sla_sharpness * m)`, `m` the UE's smallest KPI margin in natural log units (`_sla_margin`: log of rate over required rate, of delay budget over delay, of failure budget over `ue.failure_rate`; -inf without RBs), and 1 for a slice without demand. The binary `info['sla_*']` then uses `m >= -sla_tolerance` instead of the v1.1 float equality. Needs `qos_aggregation: min`. The trainer takes the objective count and names from the env: with the v1.1 vector it keeps the v1.1 CSV columns and `_hv_3d`; otherwise columns are named after the objectives (binary shares as `*_share`) and HV is `_hv_nd` (exact, the same sweep recursing to `_hv_3d`; equal to it on all 400 fronts of `results-v1.1` and within the Monte Carlo CI in 4D) with reference -1.1 for energy and -0.1 for the rest. `eval_checkpoint.py`, `aggregate_seeds.py` and the other eval tools still assume 3 objectives.
- Energy is the average power draw over `T_L`, min-max normalized by `_init_energy_bounds`. Propulsion follows Zeng et al. 2019, so hovering costs more than cruising (about 1.37 kW against 0.94 kW at the best speed, about 21 m/s, which is above the 17 m/s limit). Transmit draw is `P_tx / pa_efficiency`, or EARTH's model with `radio_static_power`.
- QoS is the mean satisfaction within each slice, then weighted by `slice_weights` (equal in both configs) over the slices present, so UE counts set load, not priority. With `qos_aggregation: min` (both configs) a UE scores the worst of its rate, delay and reliability satisfactions (reliability in orders of magnitude of the failure rate), 1 exactly when its SLA holds; `qos_weights` are then unused. `info` reports the share of UEs meeting their full SLA per slice (`sla_embb`, `sla_urllc`, `sla_mmtc`, nan when a slice has no demand). mMTC's throughput term is 1 if it has RBs (`min_rate: 0`).
- `channel.link_adaptation`: the residual error is `bler_target ** max_transmissions` (0.1 with 4 HARQ transmissions for eMBB/mMTC, 1e-5 one-shot for URLLC), retransmission delay `bler / (1 - bler) * harq_rtt_ms`. URLLC's rate is the BLER-0.1 rate times a finite-blocklength ratio (`_build_fbl_ratio`) within CQI table 3. Without the section, the older SINR-based PER curve.
- Fairness is Jain's index over all active UEs with demand, with uncovered UEs at zero. Both configs set `fairness_metric: fraction_of_demand`, Jain, Chiu & Hawe 1984, Sec. 5 item 6: `x_i = min(throughput / offered load, 1)`. Without the key `x_i` is throughput over the slice's `min_rate`, uncapped; a few mMTC UEs given many RBs (up to 800x their need) then dominate it and the index falls to about 0.1.

**Agent (`MOMA_AC_Agent`).** TD3 with omega concatenated to the inputs of both actor and critics. Critic targets and losses stay vector-valued, one Q per objective; the target uses whichever twin has the lower scalarized value. Only the actor loss scalarizes (`Q · omega`), using `critic1`.

**Omega sampling (`omega_sampling`):**
- `random`: Dirichlet(1,1,1) per episode, the original MOMA-AC and the baseline. `corner_warmup_episodes` can prepend corner weights but defaults to 0.
- `ols`: same support loop and two-part update as `gpi_ls`, sharing its `gpi_*` config keys, but behaviour and evaluation use the plain actor and corner weights rank by the OLS gap (SFOLS Alg. 2): `optimistic_value` (an LP over every weight explored so far, `support.explored`, including dropped ones) minus the best measured value. It borrows only the OLS ranking: unlike SFOLS there is no separate policy per weight, no priority queue, and GPI is not used in training. It exists to test the sampling without critic estimates choosing actions.
- `gpi_ls`: GPI-PD Alg. 2 without the model. Omega is drawn per episode from the support M (`GPILinearSupport`, starts at the extrema). Every `gpi_ls_interval` steps, `update_weight_support` evaluates M, drops weights that are best at no corner weight (extrema are kept), and adds the top-k corner weights by `v^GPI_w − max_π v^π_w`. Behaviour follows the GPI policy (`gpi_select_action`, Eq. 21) plus noise. Each update trains on the batch twice: half with the current omega, half with `w' ~ M`.
- `gpi_pd`: `gpi_ls` plus GPI-prioritized replay (`PrioritizedReplayBuffer`, Eq. 10 and 17, with a Huber critic loss) and Dyna (`DynamicsEnsemble`, every `dyna_interval` steps, one-step rollouts into `model_buffer`, mixed into batches by `dyna_beta`). The ensemble members are one batched network (`EnsembleLinear`), about 4x faster on GPU than separate nets, and each member still draws its own batch.

**Several UAVs (`MOMA_CTDE_Agent`, chosen by the trainer when the env has more than one UAV).** MOMA-TD3, Alg. 1 of the MOMA-AC paper. `MultiHeadActor` has a shared trunk and one head per UAV, and `pi_i` sees only `o_i` and omega. Critics take the joint observation and joint action, flattened in UAV id order, and so does the one `buffer` (equivalent to the paper's per-agent buffers sampled in sync, since the reward is shared). The actor loss follows Eq. 10-12: for each UAV only `a_i` comes from the actor, the others keep their replay actions, and the per-UAV losses are summed. Only `omega_sampling: random` is supported; anything else raises `ValueError`. `expl_noise` decays as in the one-UAV runs, while the paper's Alg. 1 shows a fixed sigma; set `expl_noise_decay: 1.0` for that. `guide_prob` and `guide_until` turn on the formation guide (`guide.py`, any number of UAVs): in that share of episodes, falling linearly to 0 at `guide_until`, a random regular N-gon (held still or rotating) steers the movement for the first k steps (k uniform up to the episode length) while the actor keeps power and bandwidth. It only feeds exploration data, as in DDPGfD and Jump-Start RL; evaluation is always the actor. Off by default. `omega_per_agent` (thesis, default 0) gives each UAV its own omega: omega has shape (n_agents, 3), an episode draws an independent Dirichlet omega per UAV with that probability (else one shared omega, repeated), `pi_i` sees its own `omega_i`, the critics (and the buffer) take all of them flattened, the actor loss of copy i is `omega_i . Q_team`, and the lower twin of the target is picked with the mean omega. The reward stays the team vector. `omega_switch_prob` redraws omega within an episode at that per-step probability (`maybe_switch_omega`). `select_joint_action` and `eval_omega` take a team omega or one per UAV. With both keys at 0 a smoke run gives bit-identical weights to the code before them; checkpoints with `omega_per_agent > 0` have wider critics and don't load into a team-omega agent. `network: attention` (thesis, default `mlp`) replaces the networks with ones independent of N: `AttentionActor`, one actor for every UAV, whose query is UAV i's own features (`split_obs`: the 30 dims before the other-UAV block plus backhaul and wind) with `omega_i`, attending over one token per other UAV (dx, dy, dh, power, `omega_j`; power 0 masks it) plus a learned null token; and `AttentionCritic`, a transformer layer over one token per UAV (own features, action, omega), masked by own power 0, mean-pooled to the Q vector. `forward_all` computes every UAV's action in one pass (same output as the per-UAV calls to 4e-7). A checkpoint trained with 3 UAVs loads and runs in a 4-UAV env. An update (batch 256, 3 UAVs, GTX 1650) takes about 30 ms against 14 ms for the MLP networks (43 ms with two critic layers), so a 1M-step run takes roughly 40% longer.

**Formation prior (`formation_prior`, `prior.py`; on in `moma_ac_ma3_prior.yaml`).** It is a residual policy (Silver et al. arXiv:1812.06298 Sec. IV; Johannink et al. arXiv:1812.03201 Eq. 5).
- `prior.attach` wraps `step` of both `env` and `eval_env`: UAV i's movement becomes `clip(move_to(vertex_i) + a_i[0:3], -1, 1)`. The vertex is UAV i's corner of a regular N-gon of radius `prior_radius` (600 m) around the middle of the fly range, at the top altitude (one UAV: the middle), assigned in UAV id order.
- The buffer, critics and actor see `a_i`, the residual. `zero_movement_output` zeroes the movement rows of the actor's last layer, so the untrained policy is the formation; a loaded checkpoint overwrites that. Power and bandwidth have no prior.
- With a `fleet` section the n-gon is that of the active UAVs, reassigned by the Hungarian algorithm (`assign_vertices`) at each reset and one step after the active set changes.
- Can't be combined with the guide (`ValueError`). `eval_checkpoint.py` gets the wrapped env through the trainer, so it applies the prior from the train config. `eval_formation.py` and `--formation` don't use it.
- Why: on ma3 the UAV positions explain only about 15-20% of a step's QoS variance (supervised check on v1.1, `report/scripts/position_signal.py`: test R2 0.15 from the critic's input, 0.20 from the positions alone, above 0.9 for energy), so MOMA-TD3 from scratch finds only loose formations (at the QoS corner about 500 m from the triangle for `ma3_g90` and 680 m for `ma3` with the best vertex per UAV, against about 365 m with the prior) and cruises at every weight. The r=600 triangle is within 0.46% of the RMS distance of the 3-means coverage placement of the 2 km area (`report/scripts/triangle_kmeans.py`).
- Why gamma 0.9: chosen a priori from the horizon (an action's effect fades within about 2 steps, episodes are 18) and a coarse pilot; it was not tuned with the same budget for every method. The pilot's diagnosis, that with 0.99 the vector critic drifts far below the true values (TD3 paper Sec. 5.1), came from pilot checkpoints on the env before v1.1 and does not hold as a relative statement on v1.1 (`report/scripts/critic_bias.py`, 3 seeds x 3 validation episodes, 100k and 1M): with both gammas the QoS Q is about 0.6-0.9 of the value the episode's mean reward would give if kept forever, so the errors are of the same relative size, and about 10 times larger in absolute value with 0.99 (scale 1/(1-gamma)); one `ma3` seed has a negative QoS Q at the fairness corner. Q is flat over the episode's steps while the return left falls: the time limit is stored as non-terminal and the observation has no time feature, so the critic values an infinite horizon whose end it cannot see, and with 0.99 most of that value lies past step 18 (for an objective in [0, 1], the part past step 18 can reach 83-99, 91 on average over the steps, against at most 16.5 inside the episode; with 0.9 it is 1.5-9, 4.3 on average). The gamma ablation measures gamma together with that truncation.
- Results on env `v1.1` (`results-v1.1/`, 1M steps, 5 seeds, test seeds 200000+, `aggregate_seeds.py` CIs over seeds and episodes): `ma3_prior` HV 0.332 [0.324, 0.340] (seed std 0.001), the heuristic family 0.274, `ma3_prior_g99` 0.290, `ma3_g90` 0.268, `ma3` 0.176 [0.100, 0.239] (seed 3 collapses to 0.026). Each ingredient alone reaches about the heuristic family, and only both together beat it (+0.058 [+0.048, +0.068]); the effects are sub-additive (+0.092 and +0.114 alone, +0.156 together). At 100k the prior with gamma 0.99 was no better than plain MOMA-TD3, but by 1M it is. No trained policy hovers (`eval_trajectories.py`, steps 3 on): at the QoS corner `ma3_prior` shuttles each UAV back and forth along a line near its vertex, about 11.6 m/s and 360 m from it on average, the same in all 5 seeds; at the energy corner every method flies long runs at about 15 m/s with power about 0.4. The collapsed `ma3` seed 3 is the one that stands still, at the edges of the area. The eMBB SLA share is 1-2% for every policy, heuristics included, and it is the scenario, not the metric: throughput fails for 99-100% of covered eMBB UEs (median 3-4 Mbps of 50), 56% of eMBB UEs are indoor and few are covered, and the hovering triangle with all bandwidth to eMBB reaches only 4.9%. RBs of demand areas without active UEs are wasted, and one queue per UAV serves all slices, which add to it.

There are no actor snapshots: the "policies" of GPI are the one conditioned network at each weight in M. `_gpi_action_batch` and `_gpi_td_errors` score each candidate only with its own `Q(s, a, w')` instead of the full K×K sweep, for speed.

**Evaluation.** `eval_pareto_front` sweeps a fixed set of weights (corners plus seeded Dirichlet samples) on `self.eval_env`, averages reward vectors, and computes exact 3D hypervolume in `train_moma_ac.py`, plus EUM, EU over the front, and cardinality. `eval_omega` reseeds per episode with `EVAL_SEED` (common random numbers) and restores the RNG, so values are comparable across weights, checkpoints, and variants. GPI variants also log the plain actor as `*_actor` columns; compare those with the baseline to separate the effect of sampling from the effect of GPI inference. `ols` has only the plain actor, so its `hv` is already that kind of value. With several UAVs `eval_metrics.csv` and `step_metrics.csv` add `coverage`, `handovers`, `backhaul_limited`, `min_uav_distance` (the true minimum over the steps), and `mean_uav_distance` (the mean over steps of each step's closest pair). In `eval_metrics.csv` they span the sweep, with `coverage`, `handovers`, and `backhaul_limited` as a mean of episode means like the reward vector; in `step_metrics.csv` they cover only the last finished episode. Compare `ma3` with the single row `eval_formation.py` writes, which uses the same eval seeds. Every eval also records the share of UEs meeting their full SLA per slice (`info['sla_*']`, mean over steps skipping nan, then over episodes): per omega in `pareto_points.csv`, as `avg_sla_*` (over the sweep) and `wqos_sla_*` (at omega (1,0,0)) in `eval_metrics.csv`, and per corner in `eval_checkpoint.py` output (plus `<out>_<policy>_sla.npy`).

The training eval (3 episodes, the same seeds every time) is for curves only. Report results from `eval_checkpoint.py`. The closed loop is chaotic: an action perturbation of 1e-6 moves a corner's QoS by about ±0.03, so the same checkpoint gives a different HV on Kaggle's GPU, a local GPU, and CPU (0.228, 0.224, and 0.222 for ma3 at 1M), and three episodes give an HV CI about 0.06 wide. Its CIs bootstrap over eval episodes, so they don't cover variance between training seeds.

## Known pitfalls

- **Any env change invalidates earlier results.** Reward scales move, so old HV values and any baseline run outside this repo aren't comparable. Rerun all variants. The 0.228 HV quoted below and the removed `results-v0/` come from the earlier env: suburban channel, cone antenna, 20 N UAV, uncapped fairness, 60-step episodes, and 50 UEs for `ma3`. They were runs to check that training works.
- **`--checkpoint` is only a partial resume.** It loads weights and optimizers, but the step counter restarts at 0 and the buffers are empty. For a real continuation use `--resume`, which reads `<save_dir>/resume/` (written at `save_interval` and when `--max_hours` runs out). It restarts the episode, since env state isn't saved. A finished run deletes `resume/` and writes a `DONE` file.
- **`--env_config` is only partly honored on the numba path.** Without an `antenna` section, SINR comes from the numba function, whose A2G and beam-angle constants always come from `default.yaml`, at import. With one (`ma3`), `_link_gains_db` reads the env config. `_backhaul_capacity` reads the env config either way.
- **`num_das_per_slice` must stay 3.** The trainer derives `act_dim` from it, but the env hardcodes 3 distance levels per slice.
- **GPI-LS evaluation costs env steps that aren't counted.** Each iteration runs `|M| + corners` × `gpi_ls_episodes` × `eval_steps` steps on `eval_env`, and `step` only counts training steps.
- **`calculate_sinr_batch_numba` (the unused free-space version) still has the old interference model.** Only `calculate_sinr_batch_numba_a2g` is called.
- **`_separate_uavs` has limits.** It can give up silently after 100 passes, and a vertically stacked pair pushes only vertically. Neither is reachable with `ma3.yaml`, but they matter if `num_uavs` or the separation grows a lot.
- **A UAV missing from a partial action dict can be pushed for free.** It pays no energy for the push. The trainer always sends all UAVs.
- **The ping-pong observation dim stays 0.** `ping_pong_threshold` is 5 s and `T_L` is 60 s.
- **Execution is decentralized at network level only.** `o_i` includes neighbour positions and power and global UE direction counts, so deployment assumes those links.
- **A UAV using 0 RBs causes no interference but still pays full transmit energy.**
- **The formation baseline starts in place with no flight cost,** while a trained team starts from random positions.
- **Two details of `MOMA_CTDE_Agent.update` come from TD3, not the paper's equations as written:** done masking in the Bellman target (Eq. 6) and the clamp after adding noise (Eq. 1-2).
- **Reliability is lenient to the formula's label.** `_calculate_reliability_satisfaction` uses `(1 - PER)^4`, commented as "4 retransmissions", but that is the probability four transmissions all succeed. HARQ would be `1 - PER^4`, which is nearly always 1 at these packet sizes, so the formula was left as a signal for low SINR.
- **Some files and code are stale.** `config/agents/morl_default.yaml` is not loaded by anything, and neither is `ue_dynamics.patterns`. Six of the seven per-DA observation features are constant one-hots. Comments mention `train_morl.py`, `agents_morl.py`, and `visualizer.py`, which are not in this repo. The OpenGL model classes and `QueueingModel` in `utils.py` are unused.
